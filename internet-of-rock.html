What is the Internet of Rock?

The Internet of Rock is the transformation of quarries and mines from physical places into connected networks. Every machine touching the rock now generates data. Somebody collects it, transmits it, and stores it.

The short version

The rock has not changed. The equipment around it has.

For most of my career the tools were eyes, ears, and a shared radio channel. What a crew knew about a machine lived in the crew. When a good operator left, the knowledge left too.

Sensors changed that. The machine now keeps the record. That makes a quarry a network, whether anyone planned it that way or not.

The drilllogs every foot it cuts.
The crusherreports its own amp draw.
The belt scaleweighs product in motion.
The loadercounts its cycles.

The question under all of it

Who owns the signal?

Right now the answer depends on who wrote the contract.

The data coming off a connected operation is a second deposit. It sits on top of the first one. It never depletes. The producers who generate it rarely control it.

Aggregates sell for pennies per ton. The operating data may be worth far more, and almost nobody on the producer side has priced it.

The oil and gas business learned this lesson the hard way. People signed early leases they did not understand and lived with the terms for generations. Producers are lined up to repeat it with data.

Data rights are the new mineral rights. Most of the industry is signing blind.

Five checks before the next connected machine hits the pit

  1. Read the data clause like a lease.What is collected. Where it goes. Who can sell or share it. What happens when the contract or the machine ends. A missing answer is a warning.
  2. Plan connectivity as core infrastructure.It belongs in the plan from day one. It is not an add on.
  3. Keep your own copy.Hold your operating data separate from whatever goes to the equipment maker.
  4. Grow one person who can read it.Someone who knows the plant and is learning the data. The data is only worth what your people can do with it.
  5. Put operators in the room.The people who run the plant should see the agreement before anyone signs it.

Questions operators ask

Who coined the term?

I did. I am Bobby Hickey, an operations leader in mining and aggregates and the writer of Disruptive Depths. The essay that introduced it was published July 12, 2026.

Is this the same as the Industrial Internet of Things?

No. The Industrial Internet of Things describes the technology. The Internet of Rock describes what that technology does to a quarry or a mine, and it puts the ownership question first.

Who owns the data my equipment generates?

It depends on your contract. If the agreement does not say, assume the party collecting the data controls it. Ask before you sign, not after.

Does this only matter to large producers?

No. Small operators buy the same connected machines and sign the same agreements, usually with less help reading them.

Why compare data rights to mineral rights?

Both are valuable rights that owners have signed away without understanding them. A lease gets read line by line. A data clause deserves the same care.
